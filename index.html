Preliminary Research Question and Plan¶

For our project we were interested in US Healthcare and patient risks. Looking into this we started investigating medical device usage and patient care quality in the US. To do this one of the datasets we decided to use was Medical Device Recalls (https://github.com/AlyceFawole/AlyceFawole/blob/main/Medical_Device_Recalls_Updated.xlsx) which contains FDA Medical Device Recall Records from 2024 to the early October 2026 data. This dataset will be useful for our question because this is our data to directly analyze FDA regulations since these records are coming directly from the FDA and contain specifics on which medical devices do not meet FDA regulation standards. We also plan to use another dataset to measure Healthcare institution performance and/or efficiancy in patient care. Currently, we are trying to work with Compilcations and Death - Hospital level data (https://github.com/AlyceFawole/AlyceFawole/blob/main/Complications_and_Deaths-Hospital.csv) which contains data from HCUP on patient post-care complications and post-care mortality within 30 days as well as the PSI overall scores and contributing subcategory scores for each Hospital in the dataset. This dataset was chosen to give us measurable data on service provided by healthcare institutions that we intended to link and/or analyse together with said healthcare institutions FDA compliance via a connection with our Medical Device Recall dataset and the medical devices used at said healthcare institutions. We still need an additional dataset to create a clear link between the two datasets we have now. As of right now, we have a dataset directly on healthcare performace on a institution level and another dataset directly on FDA regulations sepcifically on which medical devices failed these regulations on a National level. We will need another dataset to provide the connection between national medical devices and institution level usuage for a stronger analysis. With our current data are connections are mostly broad, but we can still analyse correlations between overal healthcare performance in specific areas and the FDA failures for medical devices in those specific areas.

Primary Guiding Question: Does failures to comply with FDA regulations in medical devices affect quality of service given by healthcare instituions?

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

df_recalls["Date Recalled"] = df_recalls["year"].astype(str) + " " + df_recalls['date'].dt.strftime('%b')


# This area is shrinking our data into legible criterias, which display the devices into categories.
def group_area(area):
    area = str(area).lower()
    if "pump" in area or "infusion" in area or "administration set" in area:
        return "Infusion"
    elif "ventilat" in area or "breathing" in area or "resuscitat" in area or "anesthe" in area:
        return "Respiratory/Anesthesia"
    elif "catheter" in area or "cannula" in area or "stent" in area or "graft" in area:
        return "Catheters/Vascular"
    elif "kit" in area or "pack" in area or "tray" in area:
        return "Kits"
    else:
        return "Other"

df_recalls["Group"] = df_recalls["product_area"].astype(str).map(group_area)

# This is affecting the y-axis, we divided common categories into numerical values
df_recalls["Group Number"] = df_recalls["Group"].map({
    "Infusion": 1,
    "Respiratory/Anesthesia": 2,
    "Catheters/Vascular": 3,
    "Kits": 4,
    "Other": 5
})

# We colored our graph by color in status (similar to that in lab 6)
cols = df_recalls["status"].astype(str).map(
    lambda s: "red" if "Early" in s else "blue"
)

plt.figure(figsize=(16,6))
df_recalls.plot.scatter(x="Date Recalled", y="Group Number",
                        c=cols, alpha=.4)
plt.xticks(rotation=45)

**EDA: ** (1):Based on our scatterplot, recalls occur in all five device groups throughout the time range we looked at. The broad “Other” category has recall activity in the most months, which is expected since it combines many device types. Among the specific groups, Infusion, Respiratory/Anesthesia, and Catheters/Vascular devices show recalls recurring at a similar, steady rate with only a few gaps, while Kits appear less often early on and then show up in consecutive months in mid-to-late 2026. (Each dot represents at least one recall in that month rather than a count of recalls, so this plot shows how consistently each group is affected over time, not how many recalls it had.)This relates back to our question because recalls in hospital-used devices are recurring and spread across several device types, which gives us a broad and steady set of data to work with.

(2)Other is the most common product area with around 23 occurences in total with the main product_area within Other being Convinence Kit. Infusion Pump comes next with 12 (16 if you count the 4 “Infusion Pump Software” entries), followed by Ventilator with 7. This matters for our question because kits and pumps are used constantly in hospitals. The recall titles also show that many kit recalls come from a single faulty component, such as recalled sodium chloride or lidocaine ampules, that ends up in many different kits. One bad part can therefore trigger recalls across many products, which helps explain why kits recur so often.

(3) 187 of the 193 entries (about 97%) are “Recall Confirmed,” and only 6 are early alerts. All 6 were issued between late August and October 2026. This matters because early alerts mean the FDA is still collecting information, so the most recent months may be incomplete or change later. Older data is more reliable than the newest data, which is something we can keep in mind while delving for more data supporting our question.
